PROJECT NAME: 040 - Color Sensor Detector
PROJECT IDEA: Read red, green and blue light levels with a TCS3200 colour sensor and print the raw frequencies together with the dominant colour it sees.
HOW IT WORKS: The TCS3200 contains red, green, blue and clear photodiodes, and pins S2 and S3 choose which filter is active. The chosen filter sends a square wave out of pin 8 whose frequency rises with the amount of that colour reflected from the object. The Arduino measures each frequency with pulseIn(), converts them into percentages using a white-card calibration taken at start-up, and then names the strongest channel as RED, GREEN, BLUE or WHITE.
ADVANTAGES:
- Produces a repeatable digital signal that is not affected by ADC noise.
- One module covers the full red, green and blue spectrum.
- White-card calibration adapts the sketch to any lamp or daylight condition.
- Raw frequencies and percentages are both printed for easy troubleshooting.
- No third-party library is needed; pulseIn() is built into the Arduino core.
REAL-WORLD USES:
- Sorting coloured items on a small conveyor.
- Paint, fabric and thread colour matching tools.
- Educational kits that teach primary and secondary colours.
- Quality checks that confirm a product has the right colour.
- Colour picking for hobby robotics and interactive art.
WHAT YOU LEARN:
- How a filtered photodiode array converts colour into frequency.
- Using pulseIn() to measure an external square wave.
- Why white-balance calibration matters for sensor accuracy.
- Separating measurement, calibration and decision logic into functions.
- Comparing three normalised values to find a dominant one.
FUTURE SCOPE: The detected colour could drive a servo that drops items into the correct bin, forming a simple automatic sorter.
POSSIBLE UPGRADES:
- Remember the calibration in EEPROM so it survives a reboot.
- Add a live RGB bar graph to the Serial Monitor.
- Trigger a buzzer or LED when a specific colour is seen.
- Store a palette of previously seen colours and match against it.
REQUIRED COMPONENTS:
- Arduino Uno
- TCS3200 colour sensor module (with its illuminating LED)
- Breadboard
- Jumper wires
- Plain white card for calibration
- USB cable
- Library: None
