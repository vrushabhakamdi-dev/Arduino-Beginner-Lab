PROJECT NAME: 006 - Potentiometer LED Dimmer
PROJECT IDEA: Turning a potentiometer knob dims and brightens an LED while the Serial Monitor reports the brightness as a percentage from 0% to 100%.
HOW IT WORKS: The potentiometer forms a variable voltage divider, so pin A0 reads a value between 0 and 1023 as the knob turns. The map() function converts that reading to a 0-255 PWM duty cycle, and analogWrite() on pin 9 applies it to the LED. Because PWM switches the pin on and off thousands of times per second, the LED looks smoothly dim or bright instead of flickering.
ADVANTAGES:
- Gives smooth, step-free analogue-style control with digital electronics.
- Requires only three wires for the potentiometer plus one LED.
- map() makes the range conversion clear and easy to adjust.
- Serial output in percent makes calibration and testing simple.
- The technique works unchanged for motors, fans, and lamp brightness.
REAL-WORLD USES:
- Dimmer switches for lamps and LED lighting.
- Volume controls on radios and audio equipment.
- Brightness sliders on phone and monitor backlights.
- Speed dials for fans, conveyors, and small motors.
- Sensor-style knobs on test instruments and synthesizers.
WHAT YOU LEARN:
- Reading analogue voltages with analogRead() and the 0-1023 range.
- Generating PWM output with analogWrite() and the 0-255 range.
- Converting ranges with map().
- Understanding duty cycle and how it creates apparent brightness.
- Printing changing sensor values to the Serial Monitor.
FUTURE SCOPE: The same dimmer can control several channels, remember the last setting, or drive a MOSFET for high-power LED strips.
POSSIBLE UPGRADES:
- Add a second potentiometer to control a second LED.
- Store the brightness in EEPROM so it is kept after power off.
- Show a bar graph of brightness in the Serial Monitor.
- Use the potentiometer to control a DC motor speed instead of an LED.
