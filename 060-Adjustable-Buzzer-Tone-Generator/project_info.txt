PROJECT NAME: 060 - Adjustable Buzzer Tone Generator
PROJECT IDEA: A simple signal generator where a potentiometer sweeps a passive buzzer through frequencies from 100 Hz to 5000 Hz and a button switches the sound on and off.
HOW IT WORKS: The potentiometer wired across 5V and GND feeds its wiper into analog pin A0, giving a voltage from 0 to 5V that analogRead() converts into a number from 0 to 1023. The sketch maps that number onto 100-5000 Hz, rounds it to 10 Hz steps to stop jitter and feeds it to the passive buzzer on pin 8 with tone(). The button on pin 2 toggles the output between tone() and noTone(), and the current status and frequency are printed to the Serial Monitor four times a second.
ADVANTAGES:
- Continuous, instant frequency control with one inexpensive potentiometer.
- Rounding to 10 Hz steps removes the pitch wobble common with raw sensor data.
- A single button gives a clean on/off without changing the set frequency.
- Serial readout shows the exact frequency, turning sound into a measurement.
- The frequency range is changed with two constants at the top of the sketch.
REAL-WORLD USES:
- Testing speakers, buzzers and audio circuits over a wide frequency range.
- Calibrating hearing-test and alarm-tone equipment.
- A reference tone source for tuning and audio experiments.
- Classroom demonstrations of map() and the analog-to-digital converter.
- Base module for alarms, alert sounds and industrial signalling.
WHAT YOU LEARN:
- How a potentiometer acts as a variable voltage divider for analogRead().
- How map() converts a sensor range into a useful output range.
- How to debounce a toggle button so one press makes exactly one change.
- How tone() and noTone() control a passive buzzer.
- Why rounding sensor data improves the stability of an output.
FUTURE SCOPE: Storing several preset frequencies in memory and letting the button step through them would turn the generator into a fixed-frequency test instrument.
POSSIBLE UPGRADES:
- Add a second potentiometer to control the volume by PWM duty cycle.
- Store preset frequencies (1 kHz, 440 Hz, 500 Hz) selectable with buttons.
- Smooth the square wave with an RC filter to approximate a sine output.
- Build a simple 8-step sequencer that sweeps through a list of frequencies.
REQUIRED COMPONENTS:
- Arduino Uno
- Breadboard
- 10k ohm potentiometer
- Passive buzzer
- 1 push button
- Jumper wires
- USB cable
- Library: None
