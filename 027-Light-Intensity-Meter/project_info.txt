PROJECT NAME: 027 - Light Intensity Meter
PROJECT IDEA: Measure how bright the surroundings are with an LDR and show the value as a numeric percentage plus a five-LED bar graph.
HOW IT WORKS: The LDR and a 10 kohm resistor form a voltage divider whose midpoint is connected to analogue pin A0, so more light makes the LDR resistance fall and the voltage rise. The sketch reads that 0-1023 value, converts it into a percentage with map(), and maps it onto zero to five LEDs so the bar grows as the light level increases. The raw count, the percentage and a simple ASCII bar are printed every 200 milliseconds.
ADVANTAGES:
- Only one LDR and one resistor are needed for the sensing part.
- Both numeric and visual readouts show the same measurement two ways.
- No library required, so the sketch works immediately after upload.
- Teaches voltage dividers, which underpin many analogue sensors.
- Adjustable simply by moving the sensor or changing the LED thresholds.
REAL-WORLD USES:
- Automatic night lights and street lighting controls.
- Screen brightness adjusters on phones, tablets and monitors.
- Greenhouse and grow-light monitoring for plants.
- Solar tracker systems that follow the brightest direction.
- Indoor lighting comfort checks in offices and classrooms.
WHAT YOU LEARN:
- Wiring and reading an LDR voltage divider on an analogue pin.
- Using analogRead() and understanding the 0-1023 ADC range.
- Converting sensor counts to a percentage with map().
- Driving several outputs from an array of pins in a loop.
- Presenting the same data as numbers, text and a bar graph.
FUTURE SCOPE: The measured light percentage can become the input to a closed loop that switches a lamp on when it gets dark or aims a solar panel at the brightest point.
POSSIBLE UPGRADES:
- Switch a relay-controlled lamp on below a chosen darkness threshold.
- Draw a wider bar graph with many more steps in the Serial Monitor.
- Log light levels through the day to find the brightest hours.
- Add a push button to switch between percentage and raw display modes.
REQUIRED COMPONENTS:
- Arduino Uno
- LDR (light dependent resistor)
- 10 kohm resistor
- 5 x LEDs (any colors)
- 5 x 220 ohm resistors
- Breadboard
- Jumper wires
- USB cable
- Library: None
