PROJECT NAME: 088 - Electronic Safe
PROJECT IDEA: A model safe opened by a four digit PIN typed on a 4x4 keypad, with an I2C LCD display, a servo door and a 30 second lockout alarm after three wrong attempts.
HOW IT WORKS: The keypad rows on pins 9 to 6 and columns on pins 5 to 2 are scanned by the Keypad library, and the digits appear as asterisks on the LCD 1602 so nobody can read the code over your shoulder. Pressing # compares the buffer with the PIN held in EEPROM using strcmp(): a match swings the servo door on pin 10 to 90 degrees for five seconds, while a mismatch increases the strike counter and the third wrong entry locks the keypad for 30 seconds, beeping every 500 ms while a live countdown runs on the LCD. The PIN defaults to 1234 on the first boot and can be replaced at any time by sending N followed by four digits from the Serial Monitor, where EEPROM.update() saves it so it survives a power cut.
ADVANTAGES:
- The PIN is stored in EEPROM, so it survives a power failure or battery swap.
- Three strikes plus a 30 second alarm discourage people from guessing the code.
- Masking the digits with asterisks keeps the entered PIN private.
- Feedback is shown on the LCD, the buzzer and the Serial Monitor at the same time.
- Changing the PIN needs no reprogramming, just one short Serial command.
REAL-WORLD USES:
- Locker, cabinet and drawer access control panels.
- Model bank vault or jewellery box projects.
- Keypad entry for alarm panels and gate controllers.
- PIN protected settings menu on a machine or appliance.
- Classroom example of non-volatile memory and access control logic.
WHAT YOU LEARN:
- Scanning a matrix keypad and translating a key map.
- Storing and validating data in EEPROM with update().
- Driving a servo as a lock bolt and timing its window with millis().
- Managing three program states: locked, open and locked out.
- Combining an I2C LCD, a buzzer and Serial output for clear feedback.
FUTURE SCOPE: Adding a master PIN that only works during a short service window would let the safe be administered without revealing the everyday user code.
POSSIBLE UPGRADES:
- Add red and green LEDs for denied and granted status.
- Log every attempt with a timestamp on the Serial Monitor.
- Sound a tamper alarm when the keypad is used while locked out.
- Allow a longer PIN, for example six digits.
REQUIRED COMPONENTS:
- Arduino Uno
- 4x4 membrane keypad
- LCD 1602 with I2C backpack (address 0x27)
- SG90 micro servo (safe door)
- Piezo buzzer
- Small box or safe enclosure
- Breadboard
- Jumper wires
- USB cable
- Library: Keypad, LiquidCrystal I2C, Servo, EEPROM
DIFFICULTY: Advanced Beginner
LIBRARY REQUIRED: Keypad, LiquidCrystal I2C, Servo, EEPROM
